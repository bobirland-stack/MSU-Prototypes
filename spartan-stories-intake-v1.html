Share your Spartan story

Somewhere in the College right now, a student is wondering what an engineering degree actually turns into. A high schooler is deciding whether MSU is for people like her. An alum in a new city is looking for a familiar name in the field. Your path, told plainly in your own words, is what they are looking for.

The required part takes about fifteen minutes, and everything else is optional. Career changes, detours, and hard years belong here too. Our team reviews each story, and once yours is published we'll email you a link to your page to share with your networks.

Thank you. Your story is in.

Here's what happens next:

  1. Someone on our team reads your story. A person, not a system.
  2. Once it's published, we email you the link to your page.
  3. That email includes a ready-to-post version for LinkedIn, written from your own words, if you'd like to share it.
  4. Any time after that, one email updates your page, changes who can see it, or takes it down.

If a current student takes you up on that fifteen-minute call, we'll introduce you by email first. Nothing happens without you knowing.

Anything you shared under Between us stays between us: it goes to the team, never to your page.

Build notes for the MSU web team (not part of the user-facing form)

This mock uses only stock Drupal Webform capability: elements, conditional visibility, required states, file uploads, confirmation message, email handlers. Build it as one webform, share_your_spartan_story. One mock-only detail: the submit button here runs on a click handler because sandboxed preview environments block native form submission; the real Webform submits normally.

Elements and machine names

  • Element machine names mirror the question IDs shown in this mock, version included: q_fd_01_v2, q_opt_professor_v1, and so on. Never rename or reword an element that has submissions; add the new version and close the old. Question wording is versioned data, not copy.
  • The twelve trimmed path questions (two per path) live in the question-bank doc as rotation inventory. Swapping one in is a quarterly, versioned change by the content owner, not a dev task: clone the element pattern, new machine name.

Conditional logic

  • stage radios show one of three stage fieldsets. All three questions in the visible stage fieldset are conditionally required (required when visible); Webform supports conditional required states.
  • Along-the-way checkboxes each show an experience fieldset. Everything inside them is optional.
  • Path extras checkboxes each show one optional textarea. The A few more group is a required checkboxes element (minimum one), and each revealed textarea there is conditionally required when visible. Both are native Webform. Together with the three path questions this guarantees at least four answered questions per submission, the publish floor for a page that reads as a story rather than a caption.

Files, spam, accessibility

  • Three managed-file elements, jpg/jpeg/png, 10 MB, private file system. Photo 1 required. Strip EXIF location data on upload.
  • Honeypot module for spam; avoid CAPTCHA unless abuse forces it, since every point of friction costs real submissions.
  • Standard form accessibility: labels bound to inputs, error summary at top, keyboard operable. The mock's behavior is the spec.

Tagging, realistically

  • A voice-first path is a V2 stretch: record instead of type, with a transcript the storyteller approves before anything publishes. Publish-as-written holds because the approved transcript is the writing. Nothing in this form pretends to record audio today; the Webform ships text-only.
  • The role radios are the top-level conditional, same Webform mechanic as the stage radios one level down. The form is public for all three classes; the gate is review, not access. Invitation links (mention-loop note for faculty, Ambassadors and capstone teams for students) still carry their ref codes so sources stay visible, and the reviewer standard carries the gaming tells: coordinated batches, promotion-shaped answers, mention-stuffing. No contests, leaderboards, or counts ever attach to submitting.
  • Identity is one key for life: the email tied to the Spartan record (msu.edu NetID for students and faculty, Contact ID match for alumni). Review starts with one search of the sheet for the address. A returning submitter grows their existing page; a graduating student's page converts to an alumni page at the same URL. Nobody ever gets a second page.
  • V1 has no tagging operation. Structure captured at source does the work: stage (radio), experiences (checkboxes), era (derived from class year), department (the degree select above), consent tier, and the question IDs themselves. Any question's answers across the corpus is a query, not a tagging project.
  • One human judgment at publish: a route-to flag on the submission record (major gifts / planned giving / Careers / corporate and foundation relations / none). Ten seconds inside the read the reviewer already does. This replaces fund tags, company tags, and topic tags for V1. Planned giving is deliberately its own value: a late-career alum writing about the scholarship that made them is a different lead, for a different officer, than anything major gifts works.
  • Explicitly deferred: company normalization, topic or AI tagging, and all Drupal taxonomy administration. The stored fields keep every future option open; nothing about V1 forecloses the segmented layer later.

Submission handling

  • Email handler 1: acknowledgment to the submitter with the same content as the confirmation message.
  • Email handler 2: notification to the review inbox with all fields, including stage, experiences, and both optional checkboxes, so triage can route without opening Drupal.
  • Routing at triage, not in the form: the fifteen-minute-call checkbox goes to the student-connection owner; stay-involved plus the late-career or retired stage flags for gift-officer review. These rules live with the review team; the form just carries the signals.
  • The Between us fields (q_priv_hear, q_priv_partner) are excluded from every publish path and every export that feeds the public surface. They route separately: the hear field to a monthly leadership digest, the partner field to corporate and foundation relations. If these two routes don't have owners, remove the fields rather than collect answers nobody reads.
  • Leadership uses beyond the digest: published entries are ready material for advisory board meetings and podium remarks. A dean opening a board meeting with one alum's page, deks and quotes at hand, is the program's best internal distribution event, and it costs the dean's office a link, not a request to communications.
  • Peer growth is self-serve, not staff-routed: the confirmation page carries a Pass it on block with a prewritten invite, a copy button, and a mailto link. In Drupal this is confirmation-page HTML plus a few lines of JS (or plain mailto only, if the team wants zero script). No submission data is involved and no staff action is required.
  • The employer question is deliberately framed as positive fit ("What kind of engineer thrives where you work?") so the answer space is fit characteristics, not employer reviews. The framing is the guardrail; do not add a disclaimer.
  • Rotation example, already performed in this spec: q_opt_extra_v1 (outside the classroom) rotated to inventory; q_opt_employer_v1 rotated in. This is the quarterly motion: clone the pattern, new machine name, old element closed, never edited.
  • Webform submissions are the working store only. Weekly export (Webform CSV or a Views data export) into the canonical store. Drupal is not the system of record for this corpus.
  • One page per alum, enforced at review since V1 has no accounts: match on email, then name plus class year plus program. A second submission from the same person is an update to the existing entry, same node, same URL, change noted in the log. A resubmission must never create a second page; the permanent-link promise depends on it. This match is one operation serving three purposes: Spartan verification, duplicate detection, and CRM connection.
  • Salesforce connection, V1: a shared Excel file, not an integration. The weekly export is a spreadsheet on a shared drive; advancement services matches each row to its Contact record and adds two columns, Contact ID and donor status, then sends it back. Logging on the record can be as simple as a note with the story URL, or deferred entirely. Typed activities, volunteer-interest flags, and CFR tasks on the record are V2, once someone owns the Salesforce configuration. The story text never goes into Salesforce; the hear field stays in the leadership digest, not on the individual's record.
  • The Contact ID column gets pasted back into the canonical store every week. Thirty seconds, and it is still the most valuable field in the system: the permanent join key that makes every V2 ambition a query instead of a project.
  • Where the submitted role and city differ from the record's employment data, flag to the records team as a possible update. Never auto-write to Salesforce. A custom Story object, automation flows, or any live sync are gate two, earned by volume.

Development yield (V1: a spreadsheet and an email)

  • The match doubles as the discovery engine: once the donor-status column comes back, the rows sort into two piles. Entries by existing donors are stewardship touches; entries by non-donors carrying signals (route flag, stay-involved, give-back answers) are qualified discovery, arriving with no discovery-officer labor.
  • The V1 officer digest is an email with that week's rows in it: name, link, dek, route flag, donor status. Sent by whoever reviewed that week, to a small officer list. The intended use is one sentence in the officer's own words with the link. Nothing asks officers to produce anything.
  • Utilization in V1 is a tally column: officers reply used-it, or someone asks quarterly. Contact reports referencing a story activity in Salesforce is the V2 version. Either way, utilization, never gift attribution, is the program's development metric.

The map (captured now, built later)

  • The map opt-in checkbox and the structured city field are captured from day one so the corpus is map-ready when volume justifies building it. A pin requires the checkbox, the public tier, and a verified record; the location is the byline city geocoded to a centroid at review. The map must never disclose anything the story page does not already show.
  • Hometown is optional and sensitive by design: it powers aggregate views (county-level, for prospective-student audiences) and internal recruitment tools. It is never an individual public pin and never appears on a page without a separate ask.
  • If the College has an existing map surface when this ships, it becomes a layer on that surface, not a second map.

Source attribution

  • A hidden element (q_meta_source, Webform prepopulate enabled) captures how each submission arrived via URL parameter: launch invitations carry their own ref, the pass-it-on invite carries ?ref=peer, everything else lands as direct. This is the only instrumentation on the acquisition flywheel; without it nobody can ever say whether peer growth works. Include it in the weekly export.

Engagement reporting (alumni relations)

  • V1 is quarterly counts from the spreadsheet, handed to alumni relations in the framework they already report in (CASE's four modes): submissions as experiential engagement, the call and stay-involved checkboxes as volunteer interest, nearby and pass-it-on as communication engagement, give-back answers as philanthropic-adjacent context. Ten minutes with a pivot table.
  • V2 is typed Salesforce activities rolling into the engagement dashboard automatically, with zero logging by the team. Same numbers, no pivot table.
  • The nearby checkbox plus the city in the role field is a self-building regional event-interest list, opt-in clean. In V1 it is two columns in the same spreadsheet, sorted by city when an event is being planned.

Microsoft Forms fallback

  • If the pilot starts in Forms: the stage question branches to three sections; that part ports cleanly. Checkbox-revealed questions do not port, so experience and extra questions appear as ordinary optional questions in each branch, making the form longer but functional. Treat Forms as the two-week pilot vehicle and Webform as the target.
