A new center for engineering and digital innovation

The Leinweber Center for Engineering and Digital Innovation is a 265,000 square foot teaching and research facility rising on MSU’s East Lansing campus, funded by private philanthropy, the state of Michigan, and the university, with substantial completion expected in December 2028. The center expands the College of Engineering alongside its five partner colleges: six colleges, more than 75 research groups, and industry under one roof.

About the Leinweber Center

MSU describes the center as a convergence point for education, research, and career readiness. It is intentionally interdisciplinary: led by the College of Engineering with five partner colleges, built for the hands-on learning and research that prepare graduates for rapidly evolving fields, and rooted in MSU’s land-grant mission to use technology to improve society.

The idea

One building, six colleges

Engineering leads the center, and five partner colleges work inside it, so the social, business, legal, and communication dimensions of technology sit beside the engineering. Students from every one of the six colleges will learn and build here, and the building is designed so industry can work beside them; proximity shortens the distance between a research question and a working answer. Students work in the same building where more than 75 principal investigators run their research programs.

The design answers real pressures: the fields Michigan’s economy runs on no longer fit inside one discipline, demand to study engineering at MSU exceeds what current facilities hold, and distance between research and application costs time.

The center is designed to enable enrollment of 1,000 additional engineering students, expanding a college of more than 6,800.

Colocation

Why MSU for colocation

Any campus can offer square footage. Few can offer what sits beside it here: FRIB, the U.S. Department of Energy user facility whose heavy-ion chip testing serves industry at up to 8,000 user hours a year, alongside 85,000 square feet of materials science research, clean rooms, and microelectronics laboratories, in one academic district with six colleges under one roof.

MSU is the nation’s pioneer land-grant university, founded in 1855 as the model the others were built on, and pointing research at working problems has been its charter ever since. Today that mission looks like workforce: more than 6,800 engineering students, 1,000 more enabled by this building, and graduates who stay, with 60 percent starting their careers in Michigan.

The industries served

Disciplines essential to Michigan’s economic future

The center supports advanced manufacturing and materials for the mobility sector, microelectronics for the domestic semiconductor supply chain, and artificial intelligence and cybersecurity across industries. FRIB’s chip testing facilities already serve national defense and space electronics needs, and automotive companies remain the top employers of MSU engineering graduates.

Built through a partnership of private philanthropy, state investment, and the university’s own commitment, the center is shared infrastructure for Michigan: laboratory capacity, a workforce pipeline, and testing facilities that serve many employers rather than any single company. As the state’s industries navigate the mobility transition and the semiconductor supply chain takes root here, the center is designed as a durable asset for both, keeping talent in Michigan and putting breakthroughs to work in its economy.

President Kevin M. Guskiewicz called the Leinweber gift “an investment in people, in opportunity and in Michigan’s future.”
