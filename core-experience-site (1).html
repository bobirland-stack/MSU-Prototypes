First-Year Engineering CoRe Experience

The launchpad for Spartan Engineers.

CoRe brings together the cornerstone design courses, tutoring and advising, and the option to live among other engineers in South Neighborhood. It also puts first-year students in front of working engineers in year one. The aim is to build the habits and standards an engineering career depends on, starting in the first semester.

How CoRe works.

You are already in CoRe

Every incoming engineering student is part of the CoRe Experience. There is no application and nothing to sign up for. If you are taking EGR 100, this is for you.

Everyone thinks everyone else has it figured out

Research on first-year students finds this consistently. People consistently underestimate how much others liked talking to them. People consistently underestimate how glad someone would be to hear from them, and the effect is strongest when you barely know the person. And most people believe everyone else has a fuller social life than they do.

What that produces on a floor in October is everyone sitting in their room assuming everyone else is out, which is a lonely way to spend a term for no reason. The person you are nervous about messaging is probably having the same week you are.

Engineering housing is optional

Engineering students can choose to live together in Wilson, Wonders or Holden. Plenty of first-year engineers live elsewhere on campus or at home, and everything here applies either way.

Admission to major comes later

You are admitted to the college first and enter a specific degree program later. That step is called admission to major, it has its own credit and grade requirements that differ by major, and it is competitive. Ask your advisor in your first semester what your major requires. Knowing the target early gives you time to plan around it.Confirm

Tutoring is free and drop-in

Sunday through Thursday, 6 to 10 p.m., in C211 Wonders Hall. You can walk in without booking anything and without being behind.

What office hours are

They are set times when a professor is available without an appointment. Going once early in the semester makes it much easier to go back later.

Using the career office

The Center for Spartan Engineering works with students at every stage, including first-year students who have nothing on a resume yet.

What computer should I buy?

An entry-level current laptop is enough for first-year coursework. Most first-year work runs on any current laptop, and the Division of Engineering Computing Services publishes a recommended specification each year before term. Check theirs before you spend anything, because some majors have requirements that only start to matter in your second year. If money is tight, say so to your advisor before you buy — there are loaner and support options that are not advertised well.

Why am I being charged an engineering fee?

It is a per-credit fee on engineering courses that funds labs, computing, software licences and the equipment you use in EGR 100. It appears on your bill separately from tuition, which catches people out. If it creates a real problem, financial aid is the office to talk to, not your department.

Most of the first year runs through a handful of offices, and knowing which one owns what saves a lot of time. This guide explains how things work, and the offices below are where you go to do them.

Course planning, major requirements, admission to major, and anything about your record. Your advisor gives the official answer.

First time: You book a time and meet for about half an hour. Bring a rough list of what you want to ask. Most students leave with a plan for next semester written down.

EGR 100

Introduction to Engineering Design.

A two-credit course taken by every incoming engineering student. Teams of four work through one open-ended design problem across the semester and present what they build in December.

What happens in it.

A team you did not pick

Teams are formed deliberately, mixing intended majors and working styles. The first session covers how a team works before it covers what the team is building.

A problem carried in from practice

The brief comes from engineers who have lived with it. Real constraints, no worked example, and no single right answer.

Something you have to build

A working model or prototype by December, plus a written brief and a ten-minute defence to people who do this for a living.

Explaining your reasoning

Most of the grade is in the defence. Students consistently say this is the part that surprised them and the part that mattered later.

The design challenge

Where industry meets the first year.

Every first-year team spends the semester on one open problem carried in from working practice. Teams present what they built in December to a room that includes people who solve problems like it for a living.

The problem comes from outside

A brief written by engineers who have lived with the problem. Students find out early that a real constraint is not a hurdle someone invented to make the assignment harder.

Partners stay with it

Cornerstone Partners are involved from the brief through to judging, so the same engineers who set the problem see how teams solved it.

The field is wider than one company

Alumni across automotive, energy, aerospace, semiconductors and medical devices judge, mentor and answer the questions students send.

What a brief looks likeSample

Shortening the time it takes to inspect a component between flights.

Inspection runs on a fixed schedule, whether or not anything needs attention, and equipment sitting still earns nothing. Teams were asked to shorten it without reducing what gets checked.

  • ConstraintsNothing that adds weight. Nothing that requires removing the component. Must work with the tools a technician already carries.
  • What teams deliveredA working model or prototype, a written brief, and a ten-minute defence to practising engineers.

Paid undergraduate role

Design Coach Fellows.

An expanded teaching assistant role inside EGR 100, funded and trained by our Cornerstone Partners. Fellows work alongside the graduate teaching assistants and stay with the same design teams through the semester.

What the role involves

Fellows take four design teams for the semester and work with them inside studio time, from the first sketch through to the December showcase.

How partners support it

Consumers Energy and GE Aerospace fund the stipends and provide engineer-led coaching, so the methods these companies use on their own projects are applied to first-year design work.

What Fellows gain

Training in facilitation and running a design review, experience employers ask about directly, and paid hours that fit around a full engineering course load.

How the coaching role is designed. Coaches work inside the classroom with every team, not on request. Sections run this way show substantially lower failure rates, and the gains are largest for women and first-generation students. The roles are paid, so students who need earning hours can take them.

Applications open in March and are available to any student who has completed EGR 100. Apply or ask a question →
