Donate Equipment

Surplus lab equipment, test instruments, machine tools, and computing hardware can have a second life in Michigan State University's engineering teaching labs, research groups, and student capstone projects.

What we accept

The College of Engineering can put a wide range of donated equipment to use across its departments and labs. Commonly useful categories include:

  • Test and measurement instruments, such as oscilloscopes, analyzers, power supplies, and meters
  • Machine tools and fabrication equipment, including mills, lathes, 3D printers, and welding equipment
  • Computing hardware, including workstations, servers, networking equipment, and GPUs
  • Robotics and automation equipment
  • Imaging systems, sensors, and data acquisition hardware
  • Electronic components and development kits

Not sure whether something would be useful? Ask us. A short email describing what you have is plenty, and we'll answer quickly.

How to donate

  1. Contact us with a short description of the equipment: what it is, its condition, and roughly when it's available.
  2. We confirm the fit. We check with faculty and lab managers across the College and let you know whether we can put it to use.
  3. We handle the logistics. The College coordinates pickup or shipping, and University Advancement completes the gift-in-kind paperwork and acknowledgment.

Common questions

Is an equipment donation tax deductible?
Equipment gifts to Michigan State University, a 501(c)(3) educational institution, may qualify for a charitable deduction as a gift-in-kind. The deductible amount depends on fair market value and your tax situation. University Advancement provides gift documentation, and your tax advisor can confirm what applies to you.
Does the equipment need to be new?
No. Well-maintained working equipment is often exactly what teaching labs and student projects need. A make and model, approximate age, and a note on condition and documentation are all we need to evaluate the fit.
Will you always accept a donation?
Every offer gets a considered look and a prompt answer, but not every offer ends in yes. Lab space, program fit, and the cost of transporting, installing, or refurbishing equipment all factor into the decision, and sometimes they mean we have to respectfully pass. When we do, we'll tell you quickly, and where we can, we'll suggest another program that might put it to good use.
Does MSU pick up donated equipment?
Usually, yes. Once a donation is confirmed, we coordinate pickup or shipping with you.
