Sponsored projects

Projects take many forms

There is no single template for a sponsored project. The examples below illustrate the range; the ME 481 coordinator will work with you to shape your idea, whatever form it takes, into a one-semester scope with defined deliverables.

A current engineering problem

A design, build, analysis, or software challenge your organization is actively working on or has deferred for lack of capacity.

Example: redesign a test fixture; build an internal tool; evaluate three design alternatives

A problem you've already solved

A challenge your team previously addressed, approached independently by students as a point of comparison. Useful where confidentiality limits what can be shared about current work.

Example: a component design or process problem from a recent program

An area you're evaluating

A technology survey, feasibility study, or benchmarking analysis in a space your organization is considering but has not staffed.

Example: assess AI-based visual inspection methods; benchmark competitor approaches

A project for the community

Your organization funds a project for a Michigan nonprofit or public agency that could not otherwise commission engineering work. MSU identifies and matches the partner organization.

Example: assistive equipment for an adaptive sports program; a tool for a regional food bank

If your idea does not resemble any of these, reach out anyway. Projects are shaped collaboratively with the coordinator during scoping, and sample project briefs from past semesters are available on request.

Advisory board

The ME Capstone Advisory Board

The Mechanical Engineering Capstone Advisory Board builds alumni-to-alumni connections and creates opportunities for companies to participate in and sponsor ME 481 projects.

The best sponsored projects come out of relationships, not transactions. Alumni who have sponsored projects know how to scope a problem that fits a semester, and their experience helps new sponsors get it right the first time. The board keeps a steady pipeline of well-matched projects flowing into ME 481, keeps project work grounded in real industry problems, and connects students with the professional network they are about to join.
