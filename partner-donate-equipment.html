Before you retire equipment or software, talk to us

The instruments, hardware, and software your organization replaces each year can equip an MSU Engineering lab and start a Spartan's career — often with a tax benefit for you. The best time to tell us is before it's disposed of.

Companies retire usable equipment and software on a regular cycle — a lab refresh, an office move, an end-of-lease return, a version upgrade. A great deal of it still has years of teaching and research life left. We'd like to be on the short list of places you consider before it leaves the building.

How it works

  1. 1
    Get in touch. You handle retired equipment, or point us to whoever does — a quick note starts it.
  2. 2
    Tell us what's coming available before disposal. We reply with a clear yes or no within a few business days.
  3. 3
    We coordinate acceptance. Corporate & Foundation Relations confirms a faculty home, arranges pickup, and manages MSU's gift-acceptance process and written acknowledgment. If you intend to claim a charitable deduction, your organization determines and substantiates the item's fair-market value.
  4. 4
    We follow up periodically. A brief check-in each cycle, so a future refresh is easy to flag when it comes.

Why donate

  • Impact you can see. Your equipment goes into teaching labs and research, reaching students who'll graduate into your industry.
  • A potential tax benefit. Gifts to MSU can carry a charitable deduction; a documented educational use generally supports favorable treatment.
  • Often simpler than disposal. Responsible reuse instead of a disposal cost — and we coordinate pickup where we can.

More than one way to help

If an outright donation isn't the right fit, we work with companies on the arrangement that is — and help build the internal case for it.

Donate it — outright gift, matched to a faculty use. Educational discount — academic pricing; often easiest to approve. Cost-share — a lab has some budget; you close the gap. Loan or software grant — loans, demos, time-limited grants.

Tax & process

When MSU puts a donated item to use in its teaching and research mission, that "related use" generally supports the most favorable deduction treatment for equipment gifts — the reason we confirm a faculty home before anything moves. Software gifts work differently; we'll walk those through directly. Determining and substantiating fair-market value is the donor's responsibility, not MSU's — for gifts above IRS thresholds this can require a qualified appraisal and IRS Form 8283. MSU University Advancement issues written acknowledgment of each gift, and the reference and gift-agreement template are available under Records & paperwork.

General information, not tax or legal advice. Please consult your own tax advisor and MSU University Advancement before filing.

What to expect

We're grateful for every offer, and we want to be straight so the relationship is easy over time: we can't always say yes. Fit depends on current faculty need, space and power, condition, and a few compliance factors — a no is about timing and logistics, never a lack of appreciation. A few kinds of equipment carry ongoing costs (installation, service, facilities, staffing) that a gift alone doesn't cover; those can generally be accepted when they come with associated research or operating funding, and we'll flag that early. Either way, you'll hear back with a clear yes or no within a few business days.

How this page maps to Drupal

Supply-first model: the public page at /partner/donate-equipment makes the case and drives one action — an introduction to a company's equipment-disposal contact. There's deliberately no public needs catalog (unmaintainable, and companies won't browse it). Incoming availability and company contacts live in a gated staff workspace.

Why the pivot: chairs and program directors report a public "needs wishlist" is unmaintainable and companies won't browse it. The reverse (supply-first) flow — cultivate the disposal contact, capture what's coming available before disposal, let faculty claim from that — is lower-maintenance and matches how these gifts actually happen.
