A real-world proving ground for the future of how people and goods move.

MSU Mobility is a university-wide research, testing, and talent initiative that works across eight MSU colleges. Our campus is a 5,200-acre testbed with real roads, a real grid, and real traffic, where an idea can move from lab to open road quickly. We are open to industry, researchers, and students, and mobility is a top-three focus area for the university.

About MSU Mobility.

MSU Mobility advances talent and innovation for every form of mobility, through collaboration across eight MSU colleges, deep research, and a real-world testing ecosystem. Rooted in the university’s land-grant mission, we bring together work that would otherwise sit in separate buildings.

Mission and vision

Our mission.

MSU Mobility works to pioneer safer, more equitable, sustainable, and accessible-to-all multi-modal transportation solutions that advance society, drive the economy forward, and better prepare the mobility leaders of tomorrow.

We draw expertise from the colleges of Engineering, Business, Law, Communication Arts and Sciences, Education, Social Science, Natural Science, and Agriculture and Natural Resources.

The testbed

A whole campus you can test on, in four real seasons.

Most proving grounds are closed tracks. Ours is a working campus of 5,200 acres with urban, suburban, industrial, and rural zones, real traffic, and real weather. Because we own our roads, grid, and police department, we run a single process that moves a technology from approved to deployed quickly, so partners reach a real-world result sooner.

Getting started

How to test here.

Getting a technology onto our roads takes four steps.

1
Tell the office what you want to test. A vehicle, a sensor, a piece of infrastructure, or a full system, in a closed or open environment.
Through the Mobility office
2
Scope it together. The office works out which campus zones, seasons, and assets fit, and what a controlled or real-world deployment would look like.
One-stop coordination
3
Move through one permitting process. Because MSU controls its own roads and safety division, approvals run through a single streamlined path.
Streamlined permitting
4
Deploy and collect data. Run in real traffic and weather, with MSU researchers and students able to work alongside the project.
Real-world validation
Timelines and costs vary by project. Reach the office early and we will scope the steps, schedule, and any fees with you. Confirm process

The flagship

PoliMOVE-MSU autonomous racing.

MSU is the first and only Michigan team in the Indy Autonomous Challenge, where university teams program AI drivers for full-size race cars that reach nearly two hundred miles an hour. It is a partnership with Politecnico di Milano, led on the MSU side by Professor Daniel Morris. Racing at these speeds sharpens the perception and control that everyday vehicles depend on.

180 mph
World record for the fastest autonomous passing overtake, set at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway on September 6, 2024, where the team also won the passing competition.
Indianapolis Motor Speedway, 2024
111.2 mph
An autonomous hill-climb record at the Goodwood Festival of Speed, completing the climb in about sixty-six seconds.
Goodwood, July 2024
4 teams
Qualified for the first multi-car autonomous race at CES in Las Vegas, and MSU was one of them.

Spartan Stories

Real answers from Spartans working in mobility.

The questions students ask most, answered by MSU alumni now working in the field. Reach any of them, and others, through the mentoring network.

What does a day actually look like working on self-driving cars?

Less sci-fi than people think, and more interesting for it. I spend mornings looking at where our perception stack got confused the day before, then writing and testing fixes. The PoliMOVE work at MSU is exactly this loop, just faster. If you like finding the one weird edge case that breaks everything, you will like this job.

MO
Maya Okafor Sample
Perception Engineer, AV company
Autonomy & AI

I am in chemistry, not engineering. Is there a place for me in mobility?

Batteries are chemistry. The whole race to make EVs cheaper and go farther runs on materials science, and that is where I landed. My MSU research on battery materials is the reason I got my first interview. Do not count yourself out because your major does not say “automotive.”

DR
Diego Ramos Sample
Materials Scientist, battery maker
Electrification & energy

Can I work in mobility without writing code all day?

Yes. I work on the policy side, figuring out how cities and states should handle autonomous vehicles and who is liable when something goes wrong. It sits right between engineering and law. My sociomobility research at MSU is what showed me this path even existed.

AK
Aisha Khan Sample
Policy Analyst, state agency
People, policy & society

Everyone talks about cars. What about moving freight and goods?

That is where a lot of the real money and impact is. I work on making rail and trucking freight move more efficiently, which touches supply chains for the whole country. Logistics is unglamorous until you realize nothing you own got to you without it. MSU’s rail and logistics work is a genuine strength.

TB
Tyler Brooks Sample
Logistics Engineer, freight rail
Freight & logistics
