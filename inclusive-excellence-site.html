Inclusive Excellence · MSU Engineering

Programs, community, and recognition for engineering students at MSU.

Inclusive Excellence runs a set of programs that help engineering students find community and go further: a free summer bridge, six community offices with mentoring and events, and a scholarship night every February. All students are welcome in every one of them. This page explains what each does and how you join.

Women in Engineering

A community and a mentor, so that if engineering starts to feel like the wrong place, there are people who know otherwise and stay in touch.

Women rarely leave engineering over the coursework. They leave when they stop feeling like they belong, and this office works on that directly. WIE Connect pairs first-year students with a junior or senior for weekly contact and group meetings. WIE Rise carries that support into second year. The Society of Women Engineers chapter is open to any engineering student who wants to take part. The number of undergraduate women in the college has grown from about 450 in 2007 to around 1,400 today.

Engineering and Science Success Academy

A free summer program that prepares you before your first year begins.

ESSA brings incoming engineering students to campus before classes begin to sharpen math placement and build a group of people. Housing, meals, materials, and activities are covered. Accepted students pay nothing.

Why it exists

Math placement affects who finishes, and ESSA helps students improve it.

Where you place in math your first semester shapes the whole path to the degree. ESSA spends the weeks before classes on preparatory math and computer science, then lets you retake the placement, so you can start further along and shorten your time to graduation. Around that sit professional development, employer visits, community service, and mentors who have done the program before you.

It began in 2007 with support from the Michigan Louis Stokes Alliance and the 3M Foundation, and became a permanent, college-funded program in 2012. Marathon later committed $150,000 over three years to grow it.

  • 73% raised their math placementIn the 2024 cohort of 45 students, by an average of 85%.
  • 67% graduated with a degreeAcross participants from 2007 through 2018.
  • Free, and residentialYou live on campus for the program; housing, meals, and materials are all covered.
  • Mentors who’ve been through itUpper-class students and ESSA alumni are on-site the whole time.

“I originally scored a 15 on my placement exam. After ESSA, I scored a 21. It is those same people I studied with in ESSA that I study with now.”

Free. For accepted students, housing, meals, course materials, activities, and outings are all paid for.

Incoming first-year engineering students. Many are the first in their family to head into engineering, and all incoming students may apply.
How to apply
A short application form, accepted on a rolling basis until spots fill. Any demographic questions are for internal metrics and do not affect selection.

How to read these numbers

These figures describe what happened alongside the programs, and they are worth reading with care. A number like the ESSA graduation rate shows how participants did, not how they would have done without the program, because there is no control group. The programs are one part of a student’s path among many. The honest claim is that these students took part and these outcomes followed, and that the college has kept investing in the programs because the pattern has held. Where a figure would be useful but has not been published, this page marks it Add rather than estimating.

Two measures would strengthen this page most: a retention rate for ESSA participants against the college average, and where alumni of these programs are now. Both are things the office can produce from its own records. The alumni outcomes would also fill the profiles on the and pages.

Get involved

Ways to support students, whatever time you have.

This page is for two audiences: students looking for a way to get involved, and alumni, companies, and friends of the college looking to support current students. Each option below lists what it requires.

For students

Common questions from students.

The barrier is rarely the paperwork. It is usually the quiet worry that you would be out of place. Here is the plain version.

Is this open to me if I am not in that group?
Yes. Every program is open to every engineering student. The community names say who each program was built with, not who is allowed in. You will not be turned away, and events, programs, and services are open to all students.
Does ESSA cost anything?
No. For accepted students, housing, meals, course materials, and activities are covered. The most common reason people skip it is assuming there is a catch. There is not.
I do not have a 4.0. Can I still be recognized at the Showcase?
Yes. The Showcase of Stars weighs how you engage with this community alongside your grades. The awards are not reserved for the highest GPAs.
I am the first in my family to do this. Where do I start?
First-Generation Student Initiatives exists for that. The questions nobody explained are the ones they are there to answer, and asking early is what puts you ahead, not behind.
Do I have to commit to something big to join?
No. Most students start by coming to one SWE or program meeting, or signing up for one mentoring match. You decide how far in you go after that.

/ Women in Engineering

A community and a mentor, from your first year through your last.

Women rarely leave engineering over the coursework. They leave when they stop feeling like they belong, and this program works on that directly, with mentoring, seminars, and a network that starts before classes do.

What it offers

The concrete parts.

WIE Connect

First-year mentoring that matches you with a sophomore, junior, or senior. Weekly contact plus several group meetings each semester. Applications go out by email over the summer.

WIE Rise

A second-year, academic-year program that widens your network of academic, personal, and professional support once the first year is behind you.

Leadership Series

A four to six-week workshop each fall and spring to build and sharpen leadership skills, alongside peer-led success seminars.

The practical part

How it works, and how to join.

Who it is for
Any engineering student. The program was built alongside women in the college, and every event and activity is open to all students who want to take part.
How to join
Watch for the WIE Connect application by email in the summer, or come to any SWE meeting. WIE Rise information is announced at the start of each semester.
The scale
The number of undergraduate women in the college has grown from about 450 in 2007 to around 1,400 today, with retention reported at roughly 85 to 90 percent.
Recognition
WIE is a WEPAN 30-for-30 Institutional Champion, a national recognition for its work supporting women in engineering.

/ Veterans and Military-Connected Students

Community, and staff who understand the move from service to school.

Going from military life to a full engineering load is its own kind of hard. This program builds community and support for more than 200 Spartan engineers who are veterans or military-connected.

What it offers

The concrete parts.

Community and space

Events and a dedicated gathering space, plus staff who showcase the successes of other Spartans with similar experiences.

A dedicated scholarship

A Showcase of Stars veterans scholarship, awarded to student veterans and military-connected students who apply and are selected.

Trained support

All Inclusive Excellence professional staff are trained by a VITAL coordinator, a Veterans Affairs resource on supporting student veterans.

The practical part

How it works, and how to join.

Who it is for
More than 200 student veterans and military-connected students in the College of Engineering.
How to join
Connect with the program coordinator and MSU’s Student Veterans Resource Center, which the office works alongside.
VITAL
Veterans Integration to Academic Leadership, the VA program that trains campus staff to support student veterans and military-connected students.
Scholarships
Awarded through the annual Dean’s Showcase of Stars to veterans who apply and are selected.

Who runs it

/ Broadening Participation K-12

Where the path starts, long before anyone applies.

Camps, tours, and open houses show kids what engineering actually is, years before a college application. More than 2,000 elementary and middle-school students have come to campus since 2016, and current MSU students run much of it.

What it offers

The concrete parts.

Introduce a Girl to Engineering

A campus event, run largely by current MSU students, that brings girls into hands-on engineering.

Girl’s STEM Day & Green and White Days

Recurring events across the year that bring K-12 students onto campus for hands-on activities with faculty and student volunteers.

Co-Ed Programming Competition

A November competition for Michigan high schoolers, emphasizing women and underrepresented students in computer science.

The practical part

How it works, and how to join.

The reach
More than 2,000 elementary and middle-school students brought to campus since 2016.
Who runs it
Program staff plus many current MSU engineering students, which makes it a direct way to get involved while you are here.
Recognition
The program’s directors won the 2025 WEPAN and DiscoverE Girl Day Award for this work.
Get involved
Volunteer as a student ambassador at a K-12 event, or ask about the traveling campaign.

Who runs it

Talk to a person.

Broadening Participation K-12 brings young students to campus through camps, tours, and events, much of it run by current MSU engineering students.
